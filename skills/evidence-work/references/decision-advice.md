# Decision Advice

Use for business or personal recommendations where generic frameworks would obscure the user's actual choice. First identify the decision, time horizon, constraints, values, and the decision that can still be changed.

For business decisions, obtain target-specific evidence about the customer and payment reason, market structure, competing alternatives, distribution, economics, operating or regulatory cost, and the user's actual assets or advantage. Do not rank options from broad market narratives alone.

For personal decisions, use the user's provided notes, history, and constraints as `user_observation`, not as externally verified fact. Identify value conflicts and real options before advising. Do not outsource the final recommendation or private-context synthesis to a subagent.

Present viable options with their trade-offs, recommend only where the evidence and the user's priorities support one, and name conditions that would reverse the recommendation. When pivotal information is absent, give the smallest next action that would make the decision better rather than a generic life or business maxim.
