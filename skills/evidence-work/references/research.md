# Source-Backed Research

Use for broad factual investigation of a company, protocol, market, or named entity. Define a research tree before collecting sources so that the final answer does not become a pile of facts.

Choose branches that answer the user's decision or stated scope. Typical branches include current business or architecture, operating metrics, capital or ownership, counterparties, market position, risks, and material changes over time. Prefer first-party disclosures, filings, source code, official documentation, primary data, and direct statements. Use secondary sources to find leads or represent external views, not to replace primary support where it exists.

Record source date and scope. Reconcile conflicts instead of selecting the more convenient statement. Distinguish management claims, reported third-party views, and your own inferences. End with the answer to the actual question, the strongest contrary evidence, and unresolved facts that could change it.

Delegate only independent branches. The main agent must reconcile terminology, definitions, dates, and conflicts across branches before drawing conclusions.
